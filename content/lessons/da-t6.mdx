Charts are arguments. Choosing the right encoding — and deleting everything else — is how analysis persuades.

## The encoding hierarchy

Perception research ranks how accurately humans read encodings:

**position (bars) > length > angle (pies) > color**

This is why "compare 12 categories" means a sorted bar chart, never a pie: humans can't compare 12 angles, but instantly compare sorted bar lengths.

## matplotlib: anatomy, then defaults

```python
fig, ax = plt.subplots(figsize=(8, 4.5))
ax.bar(region_rev.index, region_rev.values, color="#1B5633")
ax.set_title("Revenue by region, Q3 2026", loc="left", fontweight="bold")
ax.spines[["top", "right"]].set_visible(False)
ax.yaxis.set_major_formatter(FormatStrFormatter("₹%dL"))
```

Title states the **finding**, not the variables ("Revenue concentrated in West region" beats "Revenue vs region").

## seaborn: statistical plots in one line

```python
sns.histplot(df, x="order_value", bins=30)        # distribution
sns.heatmap(pivot, annot=True, fmt=".0f", cmap="Greens")  # matrix
sns.boxplot(df, x="region", y="order_value")      # spread + outliers
```

## Delete before you present

Gridlines beyond the faintest, legends when direct labels work, dual axes (almost always misleading), 3D anything. Every pixel must earn its place.
