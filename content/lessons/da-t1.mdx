Spreadsheets remain the most-used analytics tool on earth. Formula fluency and pivot-table intuition transfer directly to SQL and BI tools later.

## References: the $ that saves hours

- `A2` — relative: drifts when you copy the formula down/across
- `$A$2` — absolute: locked
- `$A2` / `A$2` — locked column/row only

```text
=SUMIFS(Sales!C:C, Sales!A:A, "North", Sales!B:B, "Jan")
=B2 * $G$1        "every price × one tax cell"
```

## Lookups: the modern way

```text
=XLOOKUP("EMP-104", Employees!A:A, Employees!C:C, "Not found")
```

`XLOOKUP` (exact by default, built-in fallback) replaced the VLOOKUP/INDEX-MATCH debate — know all three, use XLOOKUP.

## Pivot tables: analysis without formulas

Rows = one dimension, Columns = another, Values = aggregation, Filters = the slice. A business question ("revenue by region by month?") maps to a pivot in under a minute — this speed is the skill.

## Chart selection

Bar for comparing categories, line for trends over time, scatter for relationships, histogram for distributions. Pie charts: only for ≤3 slices — analysts quietly judge pie abuse.
